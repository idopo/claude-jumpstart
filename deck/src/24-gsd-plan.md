One phase at a time. `/gsd-discuss-phase` asks the gray-area questions before anything is planned and writes your decisions down.
`/gsd-plan-phase` runs a researcher, a planner and a plan checker; the checker loops until the plans would reach the phase goal, at most three revisions. That is the loop from part one, and it runs without you.
Then you read. This is where I ask for the plans as an artifact. Challenge: why this order, what did you add, what is missing. Then replan and read again until it is right. This is the same loop with you as the checker. Replanning is cheap; executing the wrong plan is not.
Example: the plan recomputed the per-session means in every phase. "Why not once, in phase one, and save a table?" Replan: one plan fewer, and every later phase reads the table. Two rounds of challenge, five minutes each, saved an afternoon of execution.
Example of a challenge that found a real gap: "which plan writes the figure the paper needs?" None did. Replan added it.
Plans are split into waves. Plans in one wave share no files, so they can run at the same time.
For small things, skip the ceremony: `/gsd-quick` gives you a commit and a state update without research or plan checks.
Bridge: Then it runs, and the loop closes on evidence.
Source: docs/GSD_GUIDE.md (core loop table, §4 waves, /gsd-quick, re-run /gsd-plan-phase to fix a plan)
'''

- `/gsd-plan-phase`: research, plans, and a checker that loops until they pass
- Read them as an artifact, challenge, and replan until right
- Small job? `/gsd-quick` skips research and plan checks
